Guides

Prep Time: 47 hours Cook Time: 40 minutes Total Time: 48 hours Difficulty: Hard Source: @mattjcamp

Ingredients

Starter


½ tbsp sourdough discard, optional, can substitute yeast

pinch yeast, optional, can substitute sourdough discard

100g water

100g unbleached flour


Sour Country Loaf


100g starter, 20% of flour

375g lukewarm water, 75% of flour, (only use one water ingredient)

450g lukewarm water (for whole grain), 90% of flour, (only use one water ingredient)

500g unbleached flour, can mix flours

10g kosher salt, 2% of flour

1 ½g yeast, 0.3% of flour, optional only needed if not using sourdough starter


Everyday Loaf


100g starter, 20% of flour

308g lukewarm water, 62% of flour, (only use one water ingredient)

370g lukewarm water (for whole grain), 74% of flour, (only use one water ingredient)

500g unbleached flour, can mix flours

15g salt, 3% of flour

2g yeast, optional if using sourdough starter, 0.4% of flour


Addins: Cinnamon Raisin


25g raisins, 5% of flour

75g brown sugar, 15% of flour

10g cinnamon, 2% of flour

½g salt, 0.1% of flour

Directions

Make Starter (Evening Before: 5 minutes)

On the night before baking, add one tablespoon of sourdough starter or a pinch of yeast to a container

Mix with the flour and water

Let the mixture sit on the counter overnight for about 12 hours

Hydrate Dough (Next Morning: 45 minutes)

Combine the water and starter into a large mixing bowl to disperse

Add flour and remaining ingredients and mix to combine and let this mixture rest for ~45 minutes

Bulk Rise (Morning: 4 hours)

Do a total of 4 stretches and folds every 30 minutes for the next two hours

Let the dough rest for the remaining two hours

Bench Shaping (Afternoon: 30-60 minutes)

Lightly flour the outside of the dough and then cut the dough into sizes for baking

Flour the remaining exposed dough and then work the dough into taunt balls

Cover and let dough balls rest for 30 minutes

NOTE If the dough turns into a flat pancake dripping too much, repeat the shaping step
to build more strength

Final Shaping (Afternoon: 10 minutes)

Lightly flour dough on top surfaces and flip the dough so the top rests on the work
surface. Take care not to deflate the dough to preserve the oven spring. We are making a
series of folds to create tension in the dough

Shape the dough using a variation of the envelope method depending on the shape that you want

Line the shaping bowl with flour and gently place the bowl seam side up into proofing bowls. Pinch the loaf shut if needed to increase tension.

Final Rise (Late Afternoon/Next Morning: 2-48 hours)

Rest for at least 2 hours at room temperature before baking

Alternatively, put in the fridge to cold ferment for up to 48 hours to maximize complexity and slightly more acidic flavor

Bake (Late Afternoon/Next Morning (1 hour)

Preheat the larger oven to 500 degrees with dutch oven inside 20 minutes before you are ready to bake. Place a rack in the middle with pizza steal between dutch oven and the heating element.

Take the dough out of the refrigerator and place dough in the preheated dutch oven

Add rice flour, score surface of dough, and replace dutch oven cover before placing into the oven

Reduce oven temperature to 450F and bake for 20 minutes

Remove dutch oven cover and then bake for another 20 minutes (optionally check after
15 minutes if you want a softer crust)

Check exterior color and then remove from oven and cool on rack. If the loaf doesn’t appear dark enough leave it in the oven after turning it oﬀ with the door open but check on it every five minutes.

Notes

We can just cut the time in half when using commercial yeast. Slap and folds can be spaced out in 20 minute intervals and we only need 3.

Active dry and instant yeast can be used interchangeably. They can be substituted for one another 1:1. We've found that active dry yeast is slightly slower oﬀ the mark than instant, as
far as dough rising goes, but in a long (2- to 3-hour) rise, the active dry yeast catches up.
